01 Intake & Scope — quote · conflicting files · confidential doc ▾
Scenario
Nadia Okonjo (Riverbend Community Health) requests a quote for Spanish, Haitian Creole and Vietnamese. Package has conflicting report versions, unclear scope, confidential material, scanned content and a later Executive Summary. Translation deadline is not explicitly stated.
People
Nadia Okonjo — client contact ES + HC linguists — available Vietnamese — 2-day lead time
Key Facts
  • FINAL 6,400 vs v3 6,180 words — later ≠ approved source
  • Financial Appendix: scanned + handwritten notes
  • Captions workbook: one tab marked DO NOT USE
  • Board Roster: CONFIDENTIAL / unrequested
  • Exec Summary: 900 words + changed figures
  • Friday board-packet ≠ confirmed translation deadline
Main Decision

Build the quote provisionally. Do not turn unconfirmed scope into a firm commitment.

Priority Reasoning

Validate gates before committing. Source approval → scope confirmation → resource feasibility → quote. Run analysis and resource checks in parallel while waiting for Nadia.

Open Questions
  • Which report version is the approved source?
  • Is the Executive Summary in scope?
  • What is the hard translation deadline?
  • How to handle scanned / handwritten content?
Immediate Actions
  • Flag source conflict and confidential file
  • Request confirmation of approved version + deadline
  • Check Vietnamese capacity against lead time
  • Prepare provisional quote ranges
"Thank you for the materials. Before finalizing the quote I need confirmation on: (1) which report version is the approved source of truth, (2) whether the Executive Summary is in scope, and (3) the hard deadline for delivery. I will hold the confidential Board Roster out of scope unless instructed otherwise."
Questions to think through
Why not quote on the latest file by default? ▾
Later modification does not equal client approval. Quoting the wrong source creates scope risk and rework.
What stays out of the quote until confirmed? ▾
Unrequested confidential content, unscannable handwritten notes, and any version not confirmed as source of truth.

02 Version Control — 31 comments · print cutoff · safety/legal ▾
Scenario
Northline print deadline is Thursday 12 PM ET. Pavel (DTP) cannot work Thursday before 1 PM. 31 client comments arrived late: clear errors, style preferences, glossary conflicts, contradictions, plus safety and legal wording changes.
People
Grant — client contact Pavel — DTP contractor
Constraints
  • Thursday 12 PM ET = hard Northline cutoff
  • Pavel unavailable Thursday before 1 PM
  • Chinese page 7 is one line from a page break
  • Safety / legal changes need written confirmation
Main Decision

Protect the print cutoff by triaging comments by risk and decision authority, not by volume.

Priority Reasoning

Hard cutoff drives everything. Resolve safety/legal first (they block DTP). Implement clear errors in parallel. Keep Wednesday for production and Thursday AM as QA buffer.

  • 2 PM — GO / NO-GO checkpoint
  • Wed PM — DTP + internal QA
  • Thu AM — QA / recovery buffer
  • Thu 12 PM ET — Northline cutoff
Controls
  • Verify source before implementation
  • Written confirmation for safety change
  • Preserve glossary consistency
  • Reflow affected Chinese pages
  • Final QA before release
"Two items need written confirmation: the safety warning and the legal line. The English master is already in prepress. Need response by 2 PM ET today to protect the Thursday deadline."
"I have six hours of Wednesday capacity. Can you confirm your window and whether you can take two waves — first batch now, second after the client decision point?"
Questions to think through
Why is comment count irrelevant? ▾
Downstream impact matters more than volume. Safety/legal changes and deadline blockers are the real constraints.
What if Grant does not respond? ▾
Ship only approved changes. Escalate unresolved decisions. Document. Do not miss the press run.

03 Capacity & Contingency — studio · silent contractor · prospect ▾
Scenario
One coordinator covering four committed projects when a high-value prospect arrives. Project D has a non-refundable Wednesday 2–4 PM studio booking requiring a final reviewed Haitian Creole script. Aristide delivered 60% and has not answered three emails since Thursday.

Main Decision

Protect existing commitments and the studio dependency. Qualify Sable Ridge without committing until Russian capacity is verified.

Priority Reasoning

Dependency rigidity drives sequence. Studio is non-refundable; next opening in 12 days. A is earliest committed delivery. B next. C is conditional. Sable Ridge is an opportunity, not a commitment.

Critical Distinctions
  • Aristide’s silence = risk, not proof of failure
  • Reviewer’s source-change comment = reported discrepancy, not verified fact
  • Sable Ridge = prospect, not a commitment
"Please confirm by 11:30 AM ET today whether you can complete the remaining script in time for review before Wednesday’s 2:00 PM recording session. If I don’t hear from you by then, I’ll activate backup sourcing to protect the studio booking."
Questions to think through
Why D before C? ▾
Studio is non-refundable; next opening in 12 days. C has longer runway and the source discrepancy can be resolved in parallel.
When does backup sourcing become necessary? ▾
If Aristide does not confirm by 11:30 AM. Silence consumes the recovery window.
What would make Sable Ridge commit-able? ▾
Russian capacity, suitability and turnaround verified — without displacing existing commitments.
