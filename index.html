Case 01 Intake & Scope

"A new client, a fast quote, and a folder of files that do not quite add up"

Coordinator's Call
BUILD THE QUOTE PROVISIONALLY, BUT DO NOT TURN UNCONFIRMED SCOPE INTO A FIRM COMMITMENT.
Why This Order

Source certainty gates scope; scope gates costing; delivery requirements and resource availability gate feasibility.

🔴 The real problem

Surface request: "send a quote today."
Actual problem: inconsistent file inventory, a confidential document sent without being requested, a version conflict between two annual report files, and a Vietnamese MT request that introduces a quality/workflow trade-off.

Working assumptions

  • A1 — Friday's board-packet date is treated as a stakeholder milestone, not a confirmed translation deadline.
  • A2 — The later modification date on v3 does not establish client approval; the approved source must be confirmed.
  • A3 — The Board Roster is out of scope unless explicitly requested.
  • A4 — The Executive Summary may represent revised/overlapping content rather than automatically adding 900 words to scope.
  • A5 — The standard linguistic workflow remains the baseline unless an alternative workflow is explicitly agreed.

Clarification questions

  • Q1 — Which Annual Report file is the approved source: FINAL or v3?
    Why: determines the correct source for translation.
    Fallback: If no answer, use v3 as the most recent version and flag the assumption.
  • Q2 — What is the actual translation delivery date/time? Is Friday the translation deadline or only the board-packet date?
    Why: determines scheduling and resource allocation.
    Fallback: If no answer, assume the board-packet date is the delivery deadline.
  • Q3 — Is the Executive Summary a separate deliverable, a replacement/update to part of the report, or additional content?
    Why: affects scope and cost.
    Fallback: If no answer, treat it as a separate deliverable and flag the overlap.
  • Q4 — Should the handwritten notes in Appendix A be included in translation scope?
    Why: handwritten content may require additional interpretation.
    Fallback: If no answer, exclude handwritten notes and flag the assumption.
  • Q5 — Which tabs/content in the captions workbook should be translated?
    Why: Tab 3 is marked DO NOT USE.
    Fallback: If no answer, translate Tabs 1 and 2 only.
  • Q6 — For Vietnamese, are you requesting an alternative QA workflow, or should we maintain the standard translation + second-linguist review process?
    Why: determines quality level and cost.
    Fallback: If no answer, maintain the standard workflow.

Case 02 Version Control & Conflicting Review

"A late client review, a legal instruction, and a print deadline that does not move"

Coordinator's Call
PROTECT THE THURSDAY PRESS CUTOFF BY RESOLVING HIGH-RISK DECISIONS FIRST AND COMPLETING ALL DTP WEDNESDAY.
🔴 The real problem

Surface request: "implement all comments + add legal warning."
Actual problem: hard print deadline (Thu 12 PM ET) + conflicting instructions + a safety/legal change that requires written confirmation before going to print. The English master is already in Northline's prepress queue.

Prioritization Logic

The binding constraint is Northline's Thursday 12:00 PM ET cutoff. Because Pavel is unavailable Thursday before 1:00 PM, production DTP must be completed Wednesday. I therefore resolve the decisions that can block DTP first and preserve Thursday morning for QA/recovery.

⚠️ If client confirmation is not received

At the go/no-go checkpoint, separate changes that are safe and ready to proceed from changes that still require client authority. Document unresolved items and escalate the schedule/print trade-off. 📌 Prospect ≠ Commitment

I will pursue the opportunity, but I will not promise Wednesday 5 PM delivery until Russian capacity and suitability are verified.

  • 1. Contact listed Russian resource.
  • 2. Verify current availability.
  • 3. Confirm fit for the language/content.
  • 4. Confirm turnaround feasibility.
  • 5. Only then offer a conditional commitment.

Commercial option: Qualify the prospect immediately and offer an alternative delivery only if the prospect is willing to renegotiate scope or timing.

TO: Manager
SUBJECT: Capacity assessment — new prospect and Project D risk

I’ve reviewed the current capacity across A–D and the Sable Ridge request.

My recommendation is Option A: protect the four existing commitments, manage Project D’s studio dependency with the 11:30 AM Aristide trigger and backup sourcing, and qualify Sable Ridge’s Russian capacity in parallel. I would keep the Wednesday 5:00 PM prospect commitment conditional until that capacity is verified.

Option B: pursue Sable Ridge at the requested deadline, but only with an explicit decision to reallocate capacity if doing so puts an existing commitment at risk.

Option C: protect A–D without exception and tell Sable Ridge that Wednesday 5:00 PM is not currently feasible, while offering an alternative timeline once capacity is confirmed.

The trade-off is straightforward: A protects existing client commitments while keeping the prospect alive; B maximizes the commercial opportunity but increases delivery risk; C minimizes operational risk but may reduce the chance of winning the account.

I recommend A.

Three Lessons, One Approach

1. Communication is the foundation
Right questions, right time, right person
2. Quality is non-negotiable
Essential controls are maintained even under pressure
3. Prioritization is about trade-offs
Decisions need clear criteria and triggers

Why this approach works for MAPA: Aligned with the company's core values—accountability, responsiveness, precision, proactivity, consistency—and demonstrates practical project management judgment.
